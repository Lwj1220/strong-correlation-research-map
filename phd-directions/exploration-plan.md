# Exploring PhD Research Directions

[Back to the research map](../README.md)

## Goal

Identify a physical question worth pursuing and the kind of research work I want to learn during a PhD. A final thesis topic is not required at this stage. Continue the current research project while using small investigations to test direction preferences.

**Provisional priority:** Mott/Kondo physics as a physical focus, effective theories as a methodological focus, and correlated topology as a comparison direction. This is an exploration order, not a claim of completed expertise.

## Three candidate directions

| Direction | Core question | Connection to current preparation | Daily research activities to try |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mott, Kondo, and correlated metals | How do electrons localize and recover low-energy coherence? | Questions about self-energy, hybridization, heavy fermions, and DMFT | Solve small models; interpret spectra and self-energies; connect predictions to measurements |
| Effective theories and many-body methods | How can degrees of freedom be reduced while preserving target observables? | Rotor discretization, projection, and interest in unitary transformations | Derive transformations; implement calculations; benchmark approximation errors |
| Correlated topology and quantum spin liquids | How can phases and excitations be identified beyond conventional local order parameters? | Interest in fractionalization, gauge structure, and fractional quantum Hall physics | Study model states, correlation and entanglement diagnostics, and the assumptions behind phase identification |

Superconductivity remains a cross-cutting phenomenon: pairing, phase stiffness, competing orders, and the normal state can connect to all three directions.

## Three-week exploration

Use relative weeks, starting when ready. Budget about one hour per day; protect the main research project's working time. Each week should produce a small calculation or derivation and a half-page reflection, rather than a large reading list.

### Week 1 — Mott/Kondo and local moments

**Question:** How can charge motion generate an effective interaction between localized spins?

- [ ] State the physical question in three sentences.
- [ ] Construct the half-filled two-site Hubbard model in the two-electron sector.
- [ ] Obtain its singlet and triplet energies analytically or numerically.
- [ ] Compare the singlet–triplet gap with the strong-coupling result J = 4t²/U.
- [ ] Plot the gap and double occupancy versus U/t; explain the regime where a spin-only description works.
- [ ] Select one paper or authoritative review on Mott/Kondo physics; record a key figure, its assumptions, and its actual observable.
- [ ] Write the weekly reflection below.

**Boundary:** A Hubbard dimer illustrates local-moment formation and superexchange. It does not establish a bulk Mott transition, Kondo screening by a continuum, or heavy-fermion coherence. Record which additional ingredients each phenomenon needs.

**Output:** One figure, one derivation or notebook, and a list of unresolved questions linking this exercise to Kondo/DMFT.

### Week 2 — Effective Hamiltonians and projection

**Question:** What is lost when high-energy states are simply discarded?

- [ ] Choose a tractable instance of the current fast/slow model and specify the retained subspace P and discarded subspace Q.
- [ ] Define the target energy window and observables before reducing the model.
- [ ] Compare the full low-energy spectrum with the spectrum of PHP.
- [ ] For a small benchmark, examine the energy-dependent correction PHQ(E-QHQ)^(-1)QHP.
- [ ] Vary the separation of energy scales and inter-subspace coupling; plot the approximation error.
- [ ] Explain why coordinate labels alone do not identify a low-energy subspace.
- [ ] Record whether a unitary block-diagonalization method has been studied, implemented, or validated; keep these statuses distinct.

**Boundary:** The energy-dependent expression requires an invertible E-QHQ. An energy-independent approximation needs a justified expansion or construction. Success in a single-particle model does not automatically establish control in a many-body problem.

**Output:** A full-versus-effective comparison, an error plot, and a precise next question.

### Week 3 — Correlated topology and spin liquids

**Question:** What evidence would distinguish a spin liquid or topological phase from an ordinary disordered state?

- [ ] Choose one concrete model and one paper or review; avoid surveying all topological phases.
- [ ] Write the proposed phase and its diagnostic before reading the conclusion.
- [ ] Trace one central figure to the quantity actually computed or measured.
- [ ] Do one bounded exercise: diagonalize a four-spin Heisenberg ring, calculate spin correlations, and compare its ground state with nearest-neighbor singlet-covering trial states.
- [ ] List what this small calculation establishes and what would require larger systems, different observables, or analytical arguments.
- [ ] Explain why a finite-system singlet, absent magnetization, or a broad spectrum alone does not prove a spin liquid.
- [ ] Write the weekly reflection below.

**Output:** One small calculation and an evidence table separating observations, model results, interpretations, and missing tests.
